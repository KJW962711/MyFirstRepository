<h1>Weird History Facts</h1>    
<p>There are so many small facts and details learned from studying history that
I find fascinating. Facts that seem absurd from a modern perspective. But were
seemingly reasonable from a contemporary perspective.</p>

<h2>Proto Germ Theory</h2>
<p>Before modern medicine most societies had a flawed view on treating illness
and disease. For example, in Ancient Egypt doctors believed that honey was a cure
all for a myriad of ailments. And in many instances they were correct. Honey is
a natural antiseptic and could be used to treat burns, wounds and infections. 
However, it was also ineffective, or even harmful in certain instances. Such as 
when used for warding away spirits, which was relatively harmless. And when
treating cavities, which was very harmful and killed many Egyptians!</p>

<h3>Everyday Poison</h3>
<p>Throughout history, there have been instances in which using seemingly
innocuous objects lead to the mass poisoning of entire civilizations. Two 
examples of this being lead in Imperial Rome and Arsenic in Victorian England. 
In Imperial Rome, lead was used for wine and food as a sweetener. It was also 
used for plumbing, construction, cookware, tools, weapons, etc. The widespread
use of lead in Imperial Rome immensely negatively impacted the health of its 
citizens. In Victorian England, arsenic was used for dying clothes. It was also
used for medicine, cosmetics, toys, wallpaper, etc. Arsenic was used extensively
in Victorian England and was the cause of many deaths and illnesses.</p>
